II.
LUDWIG SIMON OF TRIER,
EIN WORT DES RECHTS FÜR ALLE
REICHSVERFASSUNGSKÄMPFER
AN DIE DEUTSCHEN GESCHWORNEN.
FRANKFURT AM MAIN, 1849

"We had voted against the inheritability of the office of Supreme Head of the Empire; on the next day we abstained from voting. When the whole result lay before us, however, as it had emerged from the will of the majority of an assembly elected on the basis of universal franchise, we declared that we should submit. Had we not done so we should have proved that we did not fit into civil society in general."a (p. 43.)

According to Herr L. Simon “of Trier”, therefore, the most extreme members of the Frankfurt Assembly no longer “fitted into civil society in general”. Herr L.Simon “of Trier” thus appears to conceive the bounds of civil society in general as being even narrower than the bounds of St. Paul’s Church.

Incidentally, in his confession of April 11, 1849, Herr Simon had the tact to reveal the secret of both his former opposition and his later conversion.

“Cold mists have arisen from the gloomy waters of pre-March diplomacy. These mists will gather into clouds and we shall have a thunderstorm pregnant with ruin, threatening to strike first of all the tower of the church in which we are siting. Take heed and arrange for a lightning-conductor to conduct the lightning away from yourselves!”b

That is, gentlemen, it is now our skins that are at stake!
